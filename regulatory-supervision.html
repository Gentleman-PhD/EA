What ESMA will ask

Supervisory review is a known interrogation. Here's what it asks, and where the signed opinion already answers it.

ESMA ASKS
THE OPINION ANSWERS
"Who challenged this methodology, and how?"
Named. A signed opinion from a reviewer outside the team that built it, with scope, tests and findings stated. § Independence
"Where do the weightings come from?"
Sourced. Each weighting tied to its stated rationale, with the output's sensitivity to each one measured. § Weightings
"Was it back-tested where history is thin?"
Tested. Back-testing by segment, with the thin-history segments that carry the most rating impact reported separately. § Back-testing
"Do two analysts reach the same rating from the same inputs?"
Measured. Consistency across teams quantified, and any discretion the methodology allows but doesn't document identified. § Consistency
"How far can the committee overlay move a rating?"
Sized. The overlay's actual effect measured against the model it adjusts, and the cases where it outweighed the model listed. § Overlay
"Can each input be traced to its source?"
Traced. Data lineage followed past the vendor to the original source, or the break point documented. § Data lineage
"Are overrides really exceptional?"
Quantified. Override frequency and direction measured against rating outcomes. A systematic pattern is reported, not explained away. § Overrides

The questions don't change. The only variable is whether you answered them before the review, or during it.
