The Teardown: break it before you wire

The asymmetry in digital-asset allocation is that the risk model you evaluate was built by the team selling you the upside, optimised to survive the scenarios they chose to test. Smart contracts executing flawlessly do not prevent economic collapse. Real diligence means dismantling the architecture to find the exact threshold where their assumptions fail.

The second asymmetry is time. High yield and fast TVL growth at launch are almost always subsidised by terminal dilution. Day-one traction tells you nothing about whether the economics survive their own supply curve, and by the time the emission schedule outruns real demand, the early allocator is the exit liquidity.

THE WORK

We reconstruct the target's economic and risk logic from first principles, and separate the assumptions that hold in any market from the ones that only hold in a rising one. Collateral, liquidity, leverage and redemption logic are modelled as a coupled system, so liquidation cascades, de-peg spirals and reserve dislocation surface as price paths rather than footnotes.

For emission-based tokens: we project the full token lifecycle (emission schedules, staking incentives, sink-and-source flows and demand constraints simulated over time), stripping out speculative growth assumptions to locate the point at which incentives stop clearing.

WHAT YOU GET

An evidence-based read written for your investment committee: the exact solvency-failure point, the trigger conditions, your true distance to ruin, and the horizon at which the structure stops paying for itself. Free of the founder's framing and independent of the party raising the capital. You price the real risk, not the pitched one. We hold the standard; you keep the deployment decision.

SCOPE

Three to six weeks, fixed fee, agreed before work begins. Timed to your deployment, never rushed for it. Delivered and signed by the principal, no juniors, no subcontractors, no team you meet after signature.

What the pitch says / what the teardown finds

Every target clears its own first look. Here's the gap between what the deck claims and what the architecture actually does under stress.

The Pitch Says
The Teardown Finds
"Over-collateralised at 180%."
Stress-thin. Holds at today's prices; crosses below redemption obligations well before assets are exhausted.
"Audited, battle-tested contracts."
Code-safe, economically fragile. The contracts execute; the economic design fails at a price the audit never modelled.
"Sustainable yield from real demand."
Subsidised. On the current schedule, dilution outpaces demand; early capital is the exit liquidity.
"Diversified, uncorrelated collateral."
Co-dependent. Shared liquidity venues; the assets de-peg together in exactly the scenario you're underwriting against.
"Stress-tested internally."
Isolated only. Tested against single shocks, not correlated panic. The reflexive loop was assumed away.
"Compliant, regulator-ready structure."
Scrutinised. Checked against the rules that actually govern it (for a stablecoin in the EU, MiCA's reserve, liquidity and redemption requirements) by someone who ran model validation at an ESMA-supervised rating agency. Passing a code audit and surviving a supervisor are different tests.
"We already ran diligence on this."
Once, by one team. The deal that gets the lightest scrutiny is the one that breaks you, and it is never the one you expected to be light. Consistency across the pipeline is the control, not the individual review.

The pitch is built to clear your first look. The teardown is built to survive your worst case. You price the second one.

Exposure review: stablecoins and yield vaults

A stablecoin or a curated vault is a model you didn't build and can't see inside. On 10 October 2025, USDe fell to $0.65 on one exchange while holding its peg elsewhere. Weeks later, Stream Finance froze withdrawals after a $93m loss. Its tokens had been re-lent across several lending markets and made up about 65% of another stablecoin's backing. Holding five tokens isn't diversification if they share one counterparty.

THE WORK

We trace what actually backs the position: reserve and collateral composition, redemption mechanics, where the liquidity sits, and every protocol your exposure is re-lent through. Then we stress it against what broke positions in 2025: single-venue depegs, frozen withdrawals, cascading liquidations.

WHAT YOU GET

The dependency map behind each exposure, the conditions under which you can't exit at par, and the haircut you are actually carrying. What to hold stays your decision.
