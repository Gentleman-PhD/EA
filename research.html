Research Areas

How do sovereign currencies compete when a non-sovereign digital asset enters the reserve landscape? In collaboration with central-bank researchers, we extend established currency-competition models to digital reserves, informing our sovereign and regulatory advisory.

Standard risk models assume positions fail independently. We model collateral, liquidity, and redemption as a coupled system to map second-order cascades. This proprietary contagion modelling forms the mathematical engine behind our protocol teardowns and issuer stress tests.

We quantify the energy footprint, grid-emission dynamics, and environmental externalities of digital-asset networks and underlying compute infrastructure, translating physical-systems data into defensible credit and capital metrics.

Bridging traditional financial architecture with distributed ledger technology. We research the mathematical and structural limits of Real World Asset (RWA) tokenisation, ensuring traditional capital frameworks hold up under on-chain execution.

Institutional R&D Commitment

Our foundational research is conducted alongside high-level academic and central-bank partners, ensuring our baseline models are tested in the most rigorous, peer-reviewed environments. At this level, our work is built to be openly validated: explicit assumptions, stated limits, and methods documented to the standard a supervisor or journal referee demands.

While our foundational models are open and peer-legible, our applied R&D is strictly compartmentalised. When these frameworks are adapted for specific sovereign, regulatory, or institutional mandates, the resulting methodologies and tooling operate under absolute security. We maintain the same uncompromising academic standard, but the specific application remains strictly protected.

Evolutionary Dynamics of International Currencies & Non-Sovereign Assets

We supply a stochastic evolutionary model to compute the hazard rate of global currency regime transitions. By tracking decentralised agent coordination across UoA, MoE, and SoV functions, the framework maps metastable shifts between the dollar-centric equilibrium, sovereign challengers, and non-sovereign assets like gold and Bitcoin.

Option Pricing Across Closed Trading Windows

As equities transition toward continuous trading, a structural mismatch emerges with listed options restricted to regular hours, altering variance accumulation and intraday risk premiums. Lacking historical equity data, we construct an empirical proxy using IBIT, Deribit, and Bitcoin to model this novel market volatility dynamic.

Factorial Strategy Based on the Cross Section of Crypto Returns

We explore statistical factors in the cross-section of cryptocurrency returns to construct and backtest reliable portfolio strategies. By incorporating crypto-native variables like on-chain metrics and continuous trading dynamics, our rigorous framework isolates trustworthy predictive signals within the emerging digital factor zoo.

Can Crypto Be Modelled? Microstructure, On-Chain Behaviour, and Trading Reality

ETH Zürich · FinsureTech Hub · Switzerland · Zürich

Lecturer for a technical workshop detailing the quantitative realities of digital asset markets. We cover market microstructure, specifically order book fragmentation across CEX and DEX venues and volatility regime shifts. We also explore on-chain intelligence methodologies, including network forensics, behavioural modelling, and anomaly detection, alongside systematic algorithmic execution in 24/7 high-volatility environments. Finally, the session addresses regulatory compliance and risk management, highlighting why Gaussian TradFi models fail in crypto and how to optimise portfolios under strict institutional constraints.
