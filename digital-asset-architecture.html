Architecture & Stress: build it, then break it

Automated tools made tokenomics and RWA structures cheap to generate, but they all break at the same point when liquidity turns. Standard risk tools miss it: they assume positions fail independently, but shared collateral and liquidity make them fail together, in reflexive loops. Allocators have seen the clean template and know the cascade it hides. Capital follows the structure that survives the correlated drawdown.

The work

We design your token-economic and RWA structure (supply schedules, collateralisation, redemption logic, reserve, and over-collateralisation economics) to the standard an allocator's auditor applies, then we attack it.

The architecture is modelled as a coupled dynamical system, with liquidity, leverage, collateral, and incentives simulated together, so second-order effects (de-peg spirals, reflexive collateral revaluation, contagion across shared dependencies) surface before they're realised. Every guarantee is specified, parameterised, and traced to an explicit, falsifiable assumption, pressure-tested against the conditions under which it must hold, not the scenario that flatters it.

What you get

A structure that raises capital instead of stalling, and a path-dependent failure map you can put in front of a risk committee: exactly where the architecture holds, where it breaks, and your distance to that edge, documented before anyone asks.

One structure we modelled read 180% over-collateralised on paper; the coupled-system stress test found it crossing below redemption obligations at 109%, under a 55% correlated drawdown.

The Sell-Side Technical Dossier

The era of raising on a pitch deck is over. To clear an institutional risk committee, your architecture has to be independently documented, stress-tested, and verifiable on arrival. A founder speaks in vision and code, a committee speaks in boundaries and limits. That translation gap is where weeks of diligence disappear.

The work

We produce the institutional-grade model proof that lets an institutional-grade allocator, exchange, or credit committee diligence your architecture without hand-holding: model specification, parameter justification, stress results, and known limitations in one document, written to the standard their quant team expects: precise, sourced, free of promotional language. The questions they'd raise across weeks of back-and-forth are anticipated, answered, and evidenced up front.

What you get

You enter the room with technical diligence already complete. The question shifts from "can we even verify this?" to "when do we proceed?" The work is independent of the raise: we document what the model is and where it breaks, not what makes it easiest to sell.

The Diligence Matrix

Technical diligence is a known interrogation. Here's what a Tier-1 quant raises and where the dossier already answers it.

THEIR QUANT ASKS
THE DOSSIER ANSWERS
"What happens to the reserve if the primary collateral drops 60% in correlated stress?"
Mapped. The exact coverage path under correlated drawdown, with the price level where backing falls below redemption obligations. § Reserve Stress
"Where does the redemption logic actually break?"
Quantified. The trigger conditions and the precise threshold where redemptions outrun liquidity. § Failure Path
"Which assumption only holds in a bull market?"
Isolated. Every guarantee traced to a falsifiable assumption, with the ones that depend on rising prices flagged explicitly. § Assumption Ledger
"Do positions fail independently, or together?"
Modelled. Liquidity, leverage, and collateral simulated as a coupled system, the second-order cascade shown, not assumed away. § Systemic Map
"Does this survive a regulator, or just a market stress test?"
Validated. Reserve and redemption mechanics reviewed against the exact lens used to approve frameworks under ESMA supervision, not retrofitted after a term sheet is signed. § Regulatory Fit
"Is this stress-tested, or is it a pitch?"
Independent. The dossier documents where the model breaks, not what makes it easiest to sell. Written to your committee's standard. § Methodology
